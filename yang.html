Statement

Yang, for me, is about paying attention.

The engineer's job is to make things behave. The artist's job is to notice, and to hold the noticing long enough that someone else can see it too. That's what pulls me toward a camera, a moodboard, a keynote deck — and, honestly, why the Yin side ships better software: because I've spent hours studying how light moves across a face, how a room composes itself, how a story asks to be told. Attention is a transferable skill.

Process

How a Yang project moves through my hands.

01 — Read

Sit with the subject.

Before the mood board, before the shot list — a real conversation. Who is this for, what does it need to say, and what is it not?

02 — Frame

Sketch the shape.

Rough moodboards, thumbnail comps, a rhythm. The visual system before any single visual.

03 — Make

Shoot, design, iterate.

Camera in hand, cursor in Figma, deck in Keynote — whatever the medium calls for. Kaizen: small, considered passes.

04 — Ship

Deliver something usable.

Not just a folder of assets — a system the team can carry forward. Captions, alt text, file naming, the boring parts done well.
